## Strongest clauses, by company

**Uber:** the "safety features are not indicia of agency" sentence; the express-negligence wording of the damages exclusion; delegation with the class/mass waiver reserved to courts; tolling during the mandatory informal conference; binding guests and heirs to arbitration; the PAA's business-to-business framing ("you engage us") and re-confirmation on each access.

**Lyft:** "separate agreement forms between Driver and Rider"; indemnity that applies "without regard to the negligence of any party, including any indemnified person"; FAA with a named state-law fallback; detailed mass-arbitration bellwether and batching; 30-day driver opt-out with no-retaliation language; company paying arbitration-unique costs.

## Recommendations and what was applied

Applied to **both** drafts (driver agreement draft and rider-terms additions on the PR #485 branch), as drafting or enforceability techniques only, with no new fees, prices, commissions, or non-compete:

1. Safety features, standards, and deactivation are not evidence of employment, control, or agency.
2. Separate rider–driver transaction; Clemson Rides collects fares as the driver's limited payment collection agent.
3. Express-negligence language in the damages exclusion and the indemnity.
4. "Entire risk remains with you" and algorithm / location-data disclaimers.
5. Arbitration: FAA with SC Uniform Arbitration Act fallback; delegation except class/representative waiver (court decides); class waiver non-severable from arbitration; severed claims stayed until arbitration ends; tolling during the informal notice period; third parties bound; no retaliation for opt-out; Company pays arbitration fees beyond a court-level filing fee; mass-arbitration batching.
6. **No shortened claims deadline (changed from the suggested 1-year default).** S.C. Code Ann. § 15-3-140 voids contract clauses that shorten the statute of limitations, and in *Huskins v. Mungo Homes, LLC* (S.C. Dec. 11, 2024) the SC Supreme Court refused to sever a 90-day deadline and voided the **entire** arbitration agreement. A 1-year deadline would put the arbitration clause at risk, so both drafts instead say claims follow the applicable statute of limitations and the informal-notice period tolls it.
7. Cap: greater of US$100 or amounts paid (riders) / fees paid to the Company (drivers) in the 12 months before the claim.
8. Severability that replaces an invalid term with a valid term of similar effect; survival; precedence.
9. Acknowledgment that the user read, understood, and took time to consider; affirmative re-acceptance for material changes.

Driver draft only: "driver engages the Platform" framing, re-confirmation on each log-in, no shared profit or loss, no branding or uniform requirement except as law requires, proof-of-premium and renewal-copy insurance duties, workers' compensation and unemployment claims carved out of arbitration.

## South Carolina concerns for counsel

- **Exculpatory clauses.** SC strictly construes releases against the drafter and will not enforce a release of gross negligence, recklessness, or intentional conduct; a clause that tries may be struck in full. Uber and Lyft do **not** try to release their own gross negligence.
- **Express-negligence rule.** SC enforces indemnity for an indemnitee's own negligence only if stated clearly and unequivocally; the new wording is meant to meet that standard.
- **Shortened limitations period — not adopted.** S.C. Code Ann. § 15-3-140 voids contract clauses that shorten the statutory period (generally three years, § 15-3-530). *Huskins v. Mungo Homes, LLC* (S.C. 2024) held a 90-day deadline void and, because it was material and the adhesion contract lacked severability, struck the whole arbitration agreement. Source: https://www.sccourts.org/media/opinions/HTMLFiles/SC/28245.pdf (fetched 2026-10-15). The same reasoning is the main risk for any clause that tries to waive something SC law forbids, including the rider gross-negligence release.
- **Arbitration notice.** S.C. Code Ann. § 15-48-10(a) requires a first-page underlined-capitals notice if the SC Uniform Arbitration Act applies (the fallback makes this relevant).
- **FAA § 1 exemption.** Rideshare drivers on interstate airport trips (CLT is in North Carolina) may be "transportation workers," making the SC UAA fallback important.
- **SC Unfair Trade Practices Act** (S.C. Code Ann. § 39-5-10 et seq.) and unconscionability doctrine for consumer contracts of adhesion.
- **TNC Act** (S.C. Code Ann. §§ 58-23-1610 et seq.) duties on the TNC itself versus dispatch-only framing.
